Updated August 25, 2026: the Image Editor is renamed the ****Artwork Editor****, reachable post-add from the catalog card menu, decoration locks at first order, and ****product sorting within a category**** is now rep-controlled.*

Catalog Templates have left the vault and are their own source library. The Stock Vault is now ****pure extraction**** with no exception. Templates are no longer authored inside the vault — they are created from a store's existing catalog and ****capture that store's branding****. Epic A is also rewritten to match the built Add Products page, which uses one search field over a unified grid with source filter chips rather than stacked source sections with sub-tabs.*

*Amended September 1, 2026 — ****Team Profit is removed from the product.**** It is no longer an input on any surface. ****Fundraising is now the team-side markup****, and it gains a ****per-item toggle**** inside the price settings component so a rep can enable or disable fundraising item by item rather than only store-wide. Governing rule 3 is rewritten accordingly; Governing rule 1 extends the commercial lock to the new toggle and gives that lock precedence over the store-level disable; Epic J states that a bundle's fundraising ****replaces**** its members' rather than stacking with it; Epic K replaces Team Profit with Fundraising and adds bulk-pricing provenance notes. Reporting counterparts are specified in* [*Store Analytics (Reports Tab)*](https://qstrike.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TS/pages/4153049253/Store+Analytics+Reports+Tab)*.*

*Amended October 12, 2026 — ****the orphaned-category state.**** Product categories are now defined at workspace level (see *[*Product Categories*](https://qstrike.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TS/pages/4381245441/Product+Categories)*), where a category may be deleted after a warning rather than being blocked. A deletion can therefore leave products in this catalog referencing a category that no longer exists. That ****orphaned state**** is specified here: the products stay visible and purchasable, they lose storefront category placement, and the Products tab reports them as an error the rep resolves by re-filing. The state is also the trigger for ****C-27**** on *[*Store Home (Home Tab)*](https://qstrike.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TS/pages/4308860929/Store+Home+Home+Tab)*, which reads this spec's query rather than computing its own. ****This amendment also surfaces three conflicts between the store-level category model described below and the new workspace-level one; they are flagged inline in Governing rule 2 and are unresolved.***

### Problem

Reps add products mid-season, and those items must be correctly configured before customers can order them. Once items are live and players are ordering, removing them carelessly can orphan or break in-flight orders. Reps also need to revise an item's details after it is live, and design errors that reach production create disputes over who owns the mistake. Edit and removal safety cannot depend on a manual publish gate — it must be driven by what an item **is** (its source) and whether it already has real orders.

Reps also rebuild the same product sets store after store. A team that came back for a second season means re-selecting the same jerseys, shorts, and warm-ups from scratch and re-decorating every one of them, even though the gear and the branding are both identical to last year.

Profit entry compounds the problem: reps think in dollars on some gear and margin percentages on others, and a store's catalog has to express both without the rep re-deriving figures by hand or the retail formula changing meaning between surfaces. Usability testing added a further finding — reps did not distinguish **Team Profit** from **Fundraising** in practice. Two team-facing markup fields in one card produced hesitation and inconsistent entry, and reps could not articulate what the second one was for. One team-side markup, switchable per item, is what they actually needed.

Storefront presentation matters too. A shopper landing on a tab full of gear needs the required uniform first, not whatever happened to be added last — so the rep needs control over the order products appear in, not just which tab they land on.

### Solution

A single catalog surface where every product is added, configured, edited, categorized, and made available. Product intake flows through a centralized **Add Products page** presenting four sources: the **Customizer** (saved designs, saved carts, and new customizations), the **Stock Vault**, **Catalog Templates**, and products from the rep's **other stores**.

An item added to a store enters the catalog immediately as a **live store record** — there is no draft or published state — but it is **Hidden by default**. A rep makes an item buyer-facing by turning on its **Visibility** toggle, at add time or afterward. A store cannot launch until at least one product is visible.

Editing happens on a per-product **detail page** whose field enablement is governed by **Source-Based Edit Locking** (Governing rule 1). Storefront placement is governed by a required **Category / Subcategory** taxonomy (Governing rule 2), where category order determines storefront tab order and product order within a category determines storefront display order. Profit entry is governed by a single shared **price settings component** carrying **two markup inputs — Dealer Profit and Fundraising — a **`$` / `%` mode, and a per-item Fundraising toggle (Governing rule 3), used identically everywhere profit is set. **Team Required / Team Supplied** remain behavioral toggles. Product removal — not unpublishing — protects in-flight orders. Bundles, bulk pricing, and League team assignment are supported.

The **Stock Vault** is a platform-level library holding **Pro Stock** and **Sideline Stock** only. It is **pure extraction**: items come out of it into stores, and nothing is ever saved back into it.

**Catalog Templates** are a separate, rep-authored source library. A template is created from a store's existing catalog by multi-selecting items and is a **branded snapshot** of that selection — it carries the origin store's artwork with it. Templates let a rep stand a store back up with the same gear and the same decoration, without re-selecting or re-decorating anything.

#### Governing rule 1 — Source-Based Edit Locking

Every product carries a persisted **source type** set at add time, which determines its edit policy. Design editability varies by source; the commercial lock does not. A template member carries **the source type of the item it was captured from**, so a template does not launder a locked design into an editable one.

| Source | Design editable after add? | Authoring surface | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pro Stock | Yes — until first order | Artwork Editor | At add, and from the catalog until the first non-cancelled order |
| Sideline Stock | Yes — until first order | Artwork Editor | At add, and from the catalog until the first non-cancelled order |
| Template member — stock | Yes — until first order | Artwork Editor | At add, and from the catalog until the first non-cancelled order |
| Template member — customizable | Yes — until first order | Brand customizer | Once the item is in the store, until the first non-cancelled order |
| Template member — restricted source | No — permanent lock | — | — |
| Saved Design | No — permanent lock | — | — |
| Saved Cart | No — permanent lock | — | — |
| Customize | No — permanent lock | — | — |
| Other stores | Yes - until first order |  |  |

- **Design lock (restricted sources):** for Saved Designs, Saved Carts, and Custom items — made to order with strict minimum order quantities — the design can never be edited once the item is in the store. The Add Products config modal is the final point at which design is changeable.
- **Restricted-source template members inherit the lock.** An item captured into a Catalog Template from a Saved Design, Saved Cart, or Customize source deploys into the destination store still permanently design-locked. Because templates carry the origin store's branding (Epic M), such a member **can never be re-branded in the destination store**. This is the sharpest edge in the template feature and is surfaced to the rep at template creation, not discovered at deploy.
- **Commercial lock (all sources, at first order):** price settings — including the `$` / `%` mode control **and the per-item Fundraising toggle** — storefront product name, application sizing, the Team Required / Team Supplied toggles, and **decoration / artwork placement** remain editable until the item receives its first **non-cancelled** order, after which they lock. If every order against the item is cancelled, these fields re-unlock.
- **The commercial lock outranks the store-level fundraising disable.** Turning **Team Fundraising** off at store level (Settings → Store Details, or *Step 1 — Basics*) disables fundraising across the catalog, but it **cannot** disable fundraising on a commercially-locked item. An item with a non-cancelled order keeps collecting the fundraising the buyer already paid; the rep is told which items were exempted and why. The consequence is that a store may legitimately sit with **Team Fundraising off at store level while ordered items still collect it** — see Governing rule 3 and *Store General Settings*, Epic A4.
- **Decoration locks with the commercial fields.** A rep **cannot re-decorate an item that has any non-cancelled order** — re-decorating would change gear a player has already bought. This applies to both entry points into the Artwork Editor (Epic H): the **Edit artwork** card-menu action is hidden, and the item's design controls are inert. Decoration re-unlocks if every order against the item is cancelled.
- **Placement fields carve-out:** **Category, Subcategory, Visibility, Sort position, and Team assignment** (League stores) remain editable regardless of lock state, subject to two invariants — in a League store a TR/TS item can never be reduced to zero assigned Teams, and a TR/TS item's category is always Team Gear.
    - **This carve-out is what makes the orphaned state recoverable.** Category is a placement field, so a rep can re-file an orphaned product even when that product is fully locked by orders. Recovery from a workspace-level category deletion is never blocked by production state.
- **Locked-item UX:** a fully locked item's detail page renders its locked fields dimmed and non-interactive (placement fields excepted) with the banner "This item has orders — changes are locked because it is made to order." A restricted-source item with no orders shows only the design control locked, with the note "Design is locked once an item is added to the store."
- **Lock indicators on catalog cards:** a **grey lock glyph** marks an item whose design is locked; a **red lock glyph** marks an item fully locked by orders.
- **Other stores** is a browsing source, not a managed surface. The list shows products drawn from  
the rep's other stores so they can be copied into this catalog. Nothing in that list is editable  
at source, and editing is not offered there. Once a product is added to this catalog from Other  
stores it is a product of this catalog like any other — fully editable, and subject to the same  
commercial lock as any other source once it carries orders.

#### Governing rule 2 — Product Categories, Sorting & Team Settings

**OPEN — unresolved conflict with the workspace-level category model, and it must be settled before either side is built.** This rule was written when categories were created, ordered, and deleted **per store, on the catalog page**. [Product Categories](https://qstrike.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TS/pages/4381245441/Product+Categories) now defines them at **workspace level**, shared by every store, with no per-store override. Three specific contradictions follow, flagged at the bullets below: **(a)** where categories are created and deleted; **(b)** whether deleting a category reassigns products or orphans them; and **(c)** whether **Team Gear** is a protected system category or an ordinary deletable one. The third is the most consequential, because the TR/TS rules below depend on Team Gear always existing.

- **Category is required on every store catalog product** (Subcategory optional) or the storefront cannot place it. A product can nevertheless end up **without a resolvable category** if the category it referenced is deleted at workspace level — see the orphaned state below. That is a different condition from never having been categorized, and the save-time requirement is unchanged.
- **Category management.** **OPEN — conflict (a).** This spec places a **New category** field at the bottom of the catalog for inline creation, with draggable category sections whose order is the storefront header tab order. *Product Categories* places creation and deletion in **Workspace Settings** and offers no per-store list. Confirm whether inline creation on the catalog page survives at all, and — if category membership is workspace-wide — whether **section order** remains a per-store setting. Storefront tab order is plausibly still per store even when the category set is not; nothing currently says so either way.
- **Deleting a category. OPEN — conflict (b).** This spec says removing a category that contains products **triggers a reassignment prompt so products are never orphaned**. *Product Categories*, Epic D says a workspace-level deletion is **warned about but never blocked**, and that affected products **are** orphaned, with the rep re-filing them afterward. Both cannot hold. The reassignment prompt is a reasonable store-level behavior and an unreasonable workspace-level one — a deletion affecting products across many stores cannot sensibly demand reassignment inline — which is why the workspace spec chose warn-and-orphan. The orphaned state below is specified **on the assumption that warn-and-orphan wins**; if the reassignment prompt is retained instead, the orphaned state becomes unreachable and this section should be struck.
- **Product order within a category is separately rep-controlled.** Category order sets the storefront tabs; **product order within a category sets the storefront display order inside that tab**. Each product card carries a drag handle, and the catalog page's order is the order shoppers see. This is what lets a rep pin the required uniform to the top of Team Gear or surface a specific item, instead of accepting an arbitrary or date-added sequence.
    - **Subcategories group; sorting orders.** A subcategory is a grouping within its parent category's section, not an ordering mechanism. Where subcategories exist, products sort **within their subcategory**, and the subcategories themselves are ordered within the category section. Where a category has no subcategories, products sort directly within it.
    - **New products append.** A newly added product lands at the **end** of its category (or subcategory) — adding an item never displaces the placement the rep has already established.
    - **Sort position survives lock.** It is a placement field (Governing rule 1), editable at any point in an item's life, including after it has orders. Re-ordering the storefront must never be blocked by production state.
    - **Hidden products hold their position.** A hidden item keeps its sort position and resumes it when made visible; it simply doesn't render on the storefront while hidden.
    - **Orphaned products hold their position too.** An item whose category was deleted retains its sort position so that re-filing it into a category restores a sensible placement rather than appending it to the end. Where it is re-filed into a *different* category, it appends as any newly-categorized item would.
- **Two defaults ship:** **Team Gear** (system-managed, marked SYSTEM, not removable) and **Fan Gear** (an ordinary editable default). **OPEN — conflict (c), and the sharpest of the three.** *Product Categories*, Governing rule 2 states that both seeded categories are ordinary records and that the feature must work with **neither present**. That cannot coexist with the TR/TS rules below, which file every Team Required and Team Supplied item under Team Gear and lock it there. If Team Gear is deletable, then deleting it orphans every TR/TS item in every store on the workspace and leaves the TR/TS toggles with nowhere to file new ones. Three candidate resolutions: Team Gear remains protected and the workspace spec is corrected; Team Gear becomes deletable and the TR/TS rules gain a fallback; or Team Gear is deletable only while no TR/TS item references it anywhere on the workspace. Confirm before build — this is a data-integrity question, not a copy one.
- **Empty categories are hidden from the storefront.** A category with no visible product produces no storefront tab; the catalog page shows it with the note "Empty — no storefront tab until it holds a live product."
- **TR/TS ⇒ Team Gear, locked:** setting Team Required or Team Supplied files the item under Team Gear, unchangeable while either toggle is on; the rep may still set a subcategory (e.g. Uniforms, Jerseys, Pants). Subject to conflict (c) above.
- **No invisible fallback:** both toggles off means the item has no team behavior — no gate, no identity capture, no team-assignment requirement — and the rep must explicitly choose a category.
- **Toggle transitions:** turning TR/TS on files the item under Team Gear (prior category discarded, subcategory cleared) and appends it to the end of Team Gear; turning both off unlocks category and requires the rep to select one before saving.
- **One category per product.** A category holds any number of products, but a product belongs to **exactly one** category and therefore appears in exactly one category section — never in two. Subcategories group items within their parent category's section.
- **Storefront placement:** Team Gear is visible to all shoppers pre-gate; entering it prompts for the team code per the Open Access tiered gate. Both toggles on resolves as Required-wins.

**The orphaned-category state.** A product is **orphaned** when the category it references no longer resolves, because that category was deleted in Workspace Settings. It is a first-class state with defined behavior, not an error condition to be prevented — the owning spec deliberately permits the deletion that causes it.

- **The product keeps selling.** An orphaned product remains a live catalog record, keeps its Visibility state, and remains purchasable on the storefront. Nothing is unpublished, no cart is invalidated, and no order is affected. Its price, decoration, lock state, team assignment, and bundle membership are all untouched.
- **It loses storefront placement only.** With no resolvable category it appears in no storefront tab and is excluded from category filtering and category search. Shoppers can still reach it by direct link, by search on name, and through any bundle that contains it. This is a genuine degradation of the storefront and is the accepted cost of an unblocked deletion.
- **Subcategory goes with it.** Deleting a category removes its subcategories, so an orphaned product loses both. There is no parent to fall back to. *Removing a subcategory on its own is different* — those products fall back to the parent category, stay categorized, and produce no error (*Product Categories*, Epic B).
- **Error treatment on the catalog page.** Given the catalog holds orphaned products, when the Products tab renders, then those cards carry an **error state** naming the cause — the category they were filed under no longer exists — and stating that they are still purchasable but no longer placed on the storefront. The treatment is an error, not a warning: it is unambiguously wrong and requires a rep action to clear, unlike the empty-category and Incomplete-bundle notices which describe states a rep may have chosen.
- **Orphaned products are grouped, not scattered.** Because they belong to no category section, they render in a dedicated section at the top of the catalog rather than appearing at the end or vanishing from the grouped view. A rep must never have to hunt for them.
- **An addressable filter.** The Category filter carries an **Uncategorized** option scoping the catalog to the affected items, and that filter state is **addressable by link** so other surfaces can route a rep straight to it. This is what *Store Home* C-27 targets.
- **Resolution is re-filing.** The rep assigns each affected product a category, from the card or the product detail page. The error clears per product; the section disappears when the last one is re-filed. Because category is a placement field (Governing rule 1), this works on locked items.
- **Recreating the category does not help.** A category recreated with the same name in Workspace Settings is a new record with a new id, and orphaned products do not reattach to it. The rep re-files either way.
- **OPEN — to be confirmed:** whether the catalog offers a **bulk re-file** for orphaned items. A single workspace-level deletion can orphan many products across many stores, and one-at-a-time recovery is proportionate only for small cases. The catalog already supports multi-select (Epic D), but that selection is currently scoped to template creation only, so this would be the second action on it. Recommend yes.
- **OPEN — to be confirmed:** whether an orphaned product is distinguishable from one that was never categorized. Under the current save-time rules an uncategorized product cannot be created, so in principle the two cannot be confused — but that holds only while every save surface enforces category. Confirm the enforcement is total before relying on it.

#### Governing rule 3 — Price settings ($ / % mode, two markups, per-item fundraising)

Every surface that sets profit renders the same **price settings component**, with identical anatomy and identical behavior. It is specified here once; Epic E, Epic J, Epic K, and Step 2's per-item configuration modal reference this rule rather than restating it.

**Two markups, not three.** The component carries exactly two markup inputs: **Dealer Profit** (the dealer's margin) and **Fundraising** (the team's money). **Team Profit is removed from the product** — it is not an input on any surface, does not persist on any record, and has no successor field. Fundraising is the sole team-side markup.

**Anatomy.** The component is a single bordered card containing a header, one row per size band, and a footer.

- **Section header.** The label **PRICE SETTINGS** sits at the left — this exact label, on every surface. At the far right of the same header line sits the **mode control**: a **segmented two-option control,** `$` and `%`, with the active option filled and the inactive one plain. `$` is the default on every new item.
- **A row per size band.** One row for each band the product carries — **YOUTH** and **ADULT**. Each row is preceded by **its own column-header line**: **SIZE · BASE COST · DEALER PROFIT · FUNDRAISING · RETAIL**. Headers repeat per row rather than appearing once at the top, so each row reads on its own. A hairline divider separates the rows.
- **Within a row.** The band name (**YOUTH** / **ADULT**) is the row label. **Base Cost** is **read-only text, not an input**. **Dealer Profit** and **Fundraising** are text inputs, each carrying a **prefix affordance attached to its left edge** that displays the current mode symbol. **Retail** is a **derived, emphasised value at the right edge** — also not an input, and never directly editable.
- **A per-item Fundraising toggle.** The component carries a **toggle that enables or disables fundraising for this item alone**. Its exact placement is a design decision and is deliberately not fixed here; what this rule requires is that the toggle **exists within the price settings component**, is the item-level control over whether that item collects fundraising, and is present on every surface the component renders on. A new item's toggle defaults to the store's **Team Fundraising** setting.
- **Footer.** A full-width line reading **"Retail price visible to customers"** at the left, with the resolved retail for each size band at the right, each labelled by band (e.g. `YOUTH $58.13 ADULT $65.41`).

**Fundraising renders only when two flags agree.** The Fundraising column is present when **Team Fundraising is enabled on the store** (*Step 1 — Basics* / Settings → Store Details) **and** the **per-item toggle is on**. When either is off, the column is absent from every row and from the header line, retail computes without it, and **Dealer Profit is the only markup available on that item**.

- **Values persist when fundraising is off.** Turning the item toggle off — or disabling fundraising at store level — **preserves the entered fundraising amounts**. They are stored, not cleared, so a rep who turns fundraising back on finds their previous figures intact rather than having to remember them. This holds on both paths; the store-level and per-item routes behave identically.
- **Re-enabling restores prior state.** When Team Fundraising is re-enabled at store level, every item returns to **the enabled/disabled state it held before the disable**, with its preserved amount. Re-enabling is not a blanket switch-on.
- **Locked items are exempt from the store-level disable.** Per Governing rule 1, an item with a non-cancelled order keeps its fundraising. Its Fundraising column continues to render read-only alongside the rest of its locked price settings, so the item's displayed retail always reconciles with what buyers are paying. A store may therefore carry **Team Fundraising off with ordered items still collecting**; Settings reports this as a mixed state rather than a flat "disabled" (see *Store General Settings*, Epic A4).

**Zero fundraising has one canonical expression.** Switching the **toggle off** is how a rep says "this item collects no fundraising." Entering `0.00` while the toggle is on produces the same retail but is a **transient state, not a second mechanism** — the component should not be built or documented as offering two ways to express the same outcome. (This replaces the former "enter $0 or 0% in Team Profit" zero-commission mechanism, which had no field left once Team Profit was removed.)

**Behavior.**

- **The mode control informs the inputs; it does not restructure the section.** Switching between `$` and `%` leaves the rows, columns, layout, base costs, and footer structure completely unchanged. It does exactly two things: it **changes the prefix symbol on every profit input in the section**, and it **clears the entered values in every row**. No field is added, removed, converted, collapsed, or re-labelled.
- **One mode governs the whole section.** The control applies to every profit input across every row — Dealer Profit and Fundraising alike. There is no per-row, per-column, or per-field mode: a price settings section is entirely in dollars or entirely in percent, and the active segment is the single source of truth for how every input in it should be read.
- **Percent is always of base cost.** In `%` mode each input is a percentage of **that row's own base cost** — never of retail, and never of another row's base cost.
- **Retail is additive, computed per row:** `retail = base cost + dealer profit + fundraising`, resolved independently for each size band. Percentages resolve to a cent amount **per row, rounded to the nearest cent, before summing** — so the displayed retail always equals the sum of the displayed component values. Where fundraising is off, the term drops out and `retail = base cost + dealer profit`.
- **Clearing on switch is deliberate.** A figure means a different thing in each mode — `10.00` as dollars is not `10.00` as a percentage — so carrying values across would silently misprice the item. Helper copy states that switching clears the entered values. A mode switch also **clears any bulk-pricing provenance note** on the cleared inputs, since provenance on an empty field carries no meaning.
- **Bulk-pricing provenance is shown on the input.** Where a value was written by **bulk pricing** (Epic K) rather than typed here, the affected input carries a **note stating the amount was set by bulk pricing**. The note applies independently to Dealer Profit and to Fundraising. **Typing a new number in that input overrides the bulk value and clears its note** — no confirmation, no separate "override" affordance. A later bulk apply re-sets the value and the note, including over a manual override. Provenance is **persisted per item per field**, not derived at render, so it survives reload.
- **Mode is persisted per item.** Each product and each bundle stores its own mode alongside its profit values; changing one item's mode never affects another's.
- **Team Supplied** replaces the entire component with **Covered** — no rows, no inputs, no mode control, no fundraising toggle.
- **Locked state.** When the item is commercially locked (Governing rule 1), the profit inputs render dimmed and non-interactive, **the segmented mode control is non-interactive**, retaining its current selection, and **the Fundraising toggle is non-interactive** in whatever state it holds. Base cost, retail, and the footer continue to display.
- **Bundles are the one structural exception.** A bundle carries a **single profit set rather than a row per size band** (Epic J), but uses this same card, the same header-mounted mode control, its own fundraising toggle, and every behavior above — one mode for the section, percent of base cost, clear-on-switch, and the same prefix affordance.

**Where the money goes.** Fundraising entered here is added to retail and routed to the team. It is **deducted from the dealer's realized profit**, which resolves as *gross revenue minus manufacturing cost (COGS), platform fee, sales tax, and team fundraising* — the definition the workspace Overview's Dealer Profit card reports against. Dealer Profit as entered in this component is the rep's intended margin; realized dealer profit is that figure net of the deductions above.

**Worked example.** The same section in each mode, on a product with a $43.13 Youth and $50.41 Adult base cost, fundraising enabled:

| Mode | Size | Base Cost | Dealer Profit | Fundraising | Retail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `$` | Youth | $43.13 | 10.00 | 5.00 | **$58.13** |
| `$` | Adult | $50.41 | 10.00 | 5.00 | **$65.41** |
| `%` | Youth | $43.13 | 20.00 | 10.00 | **$56.07** |
| `%` | Adult | $50.41 | 20.00 | 10.00 | **$65.53** |

In `$` mode the same entry yields the same profit on both bands. In `%` mode the same entry yields **different** dollar amounts per band, because each row resolves against its own base cost — which is the reason percent mode exists. With the fundraising toggle off, the Fundraising column is absent and the same rows resolve to $53.13 / $60.41 in `$` mode.

### User persona & use cases

**Who is this for?** Store owners / reps maintaining the catalog; indirectly the team manager (coach), whose design approval is referenced.

**Scenarios encountered** Adding a mid-season item; adding several products at once; saving this season's finished catalog so the same team's store can be stood back up next year; standing up a store from a template built last season; editing a product's price, team setting, name, application sizing, or design; fixing wrong artwork on a stock item already sitting in the catalog; pricing a jersey at a flat dollar profit and a fan-gear tee at a percentage margin; setting a different margin on Youth than on Adult; running fundraising on the required uniform but not on the fan-gear tee; turning fundraising off for one item without touching the rest of the catalog; turning fundraising back on months later and finding the old amount still there; re-pricing the whole catalog at once and then overriding one item by hand; renaming the storefront-facing product name; holding a product back from the storefront until its design is finalized; pinning the required uniform to the top of a storefront tab; promoting one product above the rest of its category; **opening the catalog and finding products flagged because the category they were filed under was deleted at workspace level; re-filing those products so they return to a storefront tab;** deleting a product when players have already ordered it; deleting a product that sits inside a bundle; swapping one member of a two-item bundle for another; setting categories and ordering the storefront tabs; assigning Team Required / Team Supplied gear to specific Teams in a League store; dropping a logo onto stock gear before it goes live.

### Functional Requirements & Core Workflows

#### User Stories

- As a rep, I want new products to stay off the storefront until I say otherwise, so that customers never see a half-configured item.
- As a rep, I want to choose which brand I'm pulling product from, then add items from my saved designs and carts or build a custom one, so I don't rebuild work that already exists.
- As a rep, I want to save a store's finished catalog as a reusable template, so that next season is a few clicks rather than a rebuild.
- As a rep, I want a template to bring the decoration with it, so that repeat gear for the same team doesn't mean re-applying every logo.
- As a rep, I want to be told up front which items in a template can't be re-branded later, so that I don't discover it after deploying to a different team.
- As a rep, I want to pull an item I already used in another store, so that repeat gear doesn't mean starting over.
- As a rep, I want to drop a logo onto stock gear as I add it, so that decorated product is ready without a designer round-trip.
- As a rep, I want to fix the artwork on a stock item straight from the catalog, so that a wrong logo doesn't mean deleting the product and re-adding it.
- As a rep, I want one control that switches the whole price section between dollars and percentages, so that I am never guessing whether a given field is a rate or an amount.
- **As a rep, I want a single team-side markup rather than two, so that I am not deciding which of two fields the team's money belongs in.**
- **As a rep, I want to turn fundraising on or off for one item at a time, so that I can raise money on the uniform without marking up every T-shirt.**
- **As a rep, I want a fundraising amount I turned off to still be there when I turn it back on, so that I am not reconstructing a figure I set months ago.**
- **As a rep, I want to see which prices came from bulk pricing when I open an item, so that I know whether a number was set deliberately for this product or swept in with everything else.**
- **As a rep, I want to be told clearly when products have lost their category, so that a change made at workspace level doesn't quietly take my gear out of the storefront's tabs.**
- **As a rep, I want the affected products grouped and filterable, so that fixing them is one pass rather than a hunt through the catalog.**
- As a rep, I want edit and removal safety driven by whether an item is made-to-order and already ordered, so I never orphan a player's in-flight order.
- As a rep, I want to order the products inside a category, so that shoppers see the required uniform first instead of whatever I added last.
- As a rep, I want adding a new product not to disturb the order I already set, so that curating the storefront is a one-time job rather than a recurring one.
- As a rep, I want to see at a glance which catalog items are already in a bundle, so I understand why they aren't selectable when I build another one.
- As a rep, I want to change a bundle's members after I create it, so that a wrong or discontinued item doesn't mean rebuilding the bundle from scratch.
- As a rep, I want to control what my storefront's tabs are and what order they appear in, so the store reads the way the team shops.
- As a rep managing a League store, I want to assign each product to specific Teams (or leave it league-wide), so each player sees only the gear meant for their Team.

#### Base flow (Acceptance Criteria)

- Given I add a product, when the batch is committed, then each item enters the catalog as a **live store record** — no draft state, no publish step.
- **Visibility defaults to off.** Given any newly added item, regardless of source, then its Visibility toggle is **off**; it exists in the catalog, carries its category and lock state, and is excluded from the storefront feed.
- **Visibility is settable at add and after.** The toggle appears in the per-item configuration modal at add time, and on the product detail page thereafter. Its helper text reads "Live on the storefront once the store launches."
- **Fundraising defaults to the store setting.** Given any newly added item, then its per-item Fundraising toggle initialises to the store's **Team Fundraising** value and is editable from that point on.
- **Launch gate:** Launch Store is disabled until the catalog contains at least **one visible product**. An Incomplete bundle (Epic J) can never satisfy this gate.
- Visibility is editable in any lock state, except where a gate holds it — the render gate (Epic H) and the Incomplete-bundle rule (Epic J).
- **An orphaned product does not block launch and is not held from the storefront.** Given a visible product whose category was deleted, then it continues to satisfy the launch gate and continues to serve the storefront — losing category placement is not a visibility gate, unlike the render and Incomplete-bundle cases above.
- **Orphaned products are reported, not silently tolerated.** Given the catalog holds one or more orphaned products, when the Products tab renders, then they are grouped, carry an error state naming the deleted category as the cause, and are reachable through the **Uncategorized** filter.
- Given an orphaned product is assigned a category, then its error clears and it resumes storefront placement.

#### Team Setting Triggers (roster-optional behavior)

Setting an item to **Team Required** or **Team Supplied** triggers Open Access storefront behavior. This spec owns the workspace-side triggers; storefront, PDP, and checkout mechanics are owned by Roster-Optional Architecture and the PDP spec.

- **Identity capture (all store types):** a TR/TS item in a store with no roster (or a shopper not matched to one) collects player name, contact email and phone, and name on jersey and jersey number — personalization captured on the PDP, identity confirmed at checkout — feeding the Roster Bank. PDP jersey-number entry respects the roster's **duplicate-number policy** and its taken-number set (see Roster Management, Epic 4).
- **League stores — TR/TS must be team-assigned:** setting TR/TS requires assignment to at least one Team; the system binds the item to that Team's team code. Team assignment applies to League stores only.
- **No team setting:** items with both toggles off are fully open — no gate, capture, or assignment requirement.

**Epic A — Adding a product.** Clicking **+ Add Products** opens the **Add Products page**: a page title, a one-line description of the flow, and **a single unified product grid** filtered by source chips. There are no stacked source sections and no per-source sub-tabs — one grid, one search, one selection footer. 

- **Source pill.** A **SOURCE** pill sits above the grid showing the current brand mark, with a **Change** link opening the **"Choose a brand"** picker. The pill shows the current brand mark with a **Change** link opening the **Choose a brand** picker, whose contents come from **QX7** and reflect only the brands the rep is entitled to sell. The pill scopes the **Customizer** source and determines which brand customizer opens from Customize New Product; it does not affect Stock Vault, Catalog Templates, or other-stores results. Full gating rules live in the child page.

  The Customize New Product bullet gains a pointer to the child page and keeps its existing auto-population sentence.
- **Search.** One field spanning the grid, placeholder "Search all sources — designs, carts, vault blanks, templates, other stores." It searches across every source at once, by name or SKU.
- **Customize New Product.** A button at the right of the search row opens the brand customizer to build a product from scratch. The finished item is staged as a custom product. When the customizer opens from this flow it auto-populates Team Name, Team Colors, and Logos from the store's branding. This is a button, not a source — nothing is filtered by it.
- **Source chips.** A single row of filter chips scoping the grid, each carrying its own count, with the resolved item count displayed at the right of the row:
    - **All sources** — the default, showing everything.
    - **Customizer** — the rep's saved designs and saved carts from the selected brand. A saved cart renders as one card with a stacked-thumbnail collage and an item-count badge; selecting it stages all of its items.
    - **Stock Vault** — Pro Stock and Sideline Stock blanks. See Epic G.
    - **Catalog Templates** — the rep's saved templates, each rendering as one card with a stacked-thumbnail collage, an item-count badge, and a combined price. Selecting one stages all of its members. See Epic M.
    - **Other stores** — products the rep has already placed in their **own** stores. Selecting an item adds it at its **style block** — the original brand-customizer styling — not the customized state it holds in the other store. (Catalog Templates are the deliberate opposite: they carry the origin store's customization. A rep who wants the gear without the branding uses Other stores; a rep who wants the gear *with* the branding uses a template.)
- **Cards.** Every card shows the product image, product name, and style description, with a **+ Add to Store** action that stages that item. Multi-item cards — saved carts and catalog templates — additionally show the collage, an **n items** badge, and a combined price.
- **Configure at add time:** each staged item confirms application sizing, team setting, price settings per Governing rule 3 — **including its Fundraising toggle** — category, **visibility**, and — in League stores — team assignment, in the configuration modal. For restricted sources this is the last point at which design is changeable; the modal shows the notice "This item is made to order. Its design cannot be changed after it is added to the store, and no changes can be made after its first order."
- **Batch commit:** a sticky footer carries **Cancel** and **Confirm Selection (n)**, where **n is the running count of staged items** and is visible before the rep commits. Confirm Selection is disabled until at least one item is staged. Committed items enter the catalog as live, hidden records **appended to the end of their category** (Governing rule 2). On commit the rep lands on the store catalog, scrolled to and focusing the newly added items.
- **Same page in both entry points.** The Add Products page is identical at Step 2 of store creation and on an existing store's Products tab, including the source chips. Starting a store from a Catalog Template is therefore not a separate flow — the rep creates the store and picks the template at Step 2.

**Epic B — Availability & approval.** Items are live records on commit; there is no publish, multi-select publish, or unpublish flow. Buyer exposure is controlled solely by the Visibility toggle. **Send for Approval** is an optional post-add action in a store item's card menu (Epic D), shown only when the store has an **Active team manager**; hidden otherwise. Approval is optional and never blocks anything.

**Epic C — Removing products.** Removal is governed by two independent checks. **Source and order state** decide whether an item may be removed at all. **Bundle membership** decides what the rep is warned about and what happens to the affected bundle. Both are evaluated; an item blocked by the order check stays blocked regardless of its bundle membership.

*Order protection* — keyed to source and order state (any shopper's non-cancelled order counts):

- **Stock Vault items:** standard confirmation modal; removable regardless of order state.
- **Restricted-source, no orders:** confirmation modal; removable.
- **Restricted-source, ordered:** warning modal offering **Keep in Store** or **Go to Orders** (routes to the Orders tab to cancel the blocking order(s), after which deletion is permitted).
- **Delete Product** is also available directly on the product detail page and follows the same rules.
- **Removal never touches a template.** A Catalog Template is a snapshot taken at creation (Epic M). Deleting a product from a store's catalog does not alter, invalidate, or shrink any template that captured it.
- **Deleting an orphaned product is one legitimate resolution.** A rep who no longer wants the item may remove it rather than re-file it, under the ordinary removal rules above. The orphaned state grants no additional removal rights and imposes no additional restriction.

*Bundle protection* — keyed to whether the item is a bundle member:

- **Not in a bundle:** the standard confirmation above, unchanged.
- **Member of a bundle with three or more members:** the confirmation modal **names every affected bundle** and states that the bundle will lose that item. On confirm, the item leaves the catalog and every bundle holding it; those bundles stay complete and keep their current visibility.
- **Member of a two-member bundle:** the confirmation escalates. It names the bundle, states that removing this item leaves the bundle with a single member, that the bundle will become **Incomplete** and drop off the storefront, and offers **Cancel**, **Remove anyway**, and **Go to bundle**. Choosing Remove anyway applies the Incomplete-bundle rules in Epic J. The bundle is never auto-dissolved and its configuration is never discarded.
- **Member of an ordered bundle:** a non-cancelled order against a bundle locks its membership (Epic J), so a member cannot be removed from the catalog while that order stands. The rep is routed to Orders exactly as for any ordered restricted-source item.

**Epic D — The catalog page.** The Products tab presents the store catalog as **cards grouped into category sections**, not a flat table.

- **Header:** "Catalog", the item count, and the line "live on the storefront once the store launches", with **+ Create Bundle** and **+ Add Products** actions.
- **Controls:** a search field ("Search the catalog by name or SKU") and a **Category** filter. The filter carries an **Uncategorized** option listing products whose category has been deleted; the option is present only while such products exist, and the filter state is addressable by link.
- **Category sections:** each carries a drag handle, the category name, a SYSTEM tag where applicable, and an item count. A category section holds any number of product cards. An empty category shows "Empty — no storefront tab until it holds a live product" and prompts the rep to set that category on a product.
- **Uncategorized section.** Given one or more orphaned products, then a dedicated section renders **at the top of the catalog**, above the category sections, holding every product whose category no longer resolves. It carries an error treatment rather than the neutral note used for empty categories, states that the products are still purchasable but are not placed on any storefront tab, and prompts the rep to assign each a category. The section disappears when the last orphaned product is re-filed. It has no drag handle and no sort order of its own — it is a holding area, not a category.
- **Product cards** show the product image, product name, style description or SKU, base price, and the lock glyph. Team Supplied items display **Covered** in place of price. Clicking a card opens the product detail page. An orphaned product's card additionally carries an **error indicator** naming the cause.
- **Ordering products within a section.** Each product card carries a **drag handle**, and cards can be reordered within their category section — or within their subcategory grouping where one exists. The resulting order is the **storefront display order** inside that category's tab (Governing rule 2). The section header carries the helper line "Drag to set the order shoppers see." Reordering saves immediately and is confirmed with a toast; it is available on locked and hidden items alike.
- **Selection mode.** The catalog page supports **multi-select** across category sections — a checkbox affordance on each card, plus a **Select all** control that spans every section. With one or more items selected, a selection bar surfaces the running count and a **Create Catalog Template** action (Epic M). Selection is the entry point for template creation and — subject to the open item in Governing rule 2 — potentially for bulk re-filing of orphaned products; it does not enable bulk delete, bulk visibility, or bulk category assignment generally.
- **Card menu.** Each product card carries an overflow menu whose contents are conditional on the item's source and order state:
    - **Edit artwork** — opens the **Artwork Editor** directly on that item, with no intermediate picker, modal, or confirmation step. Shown **only** for **Pro Stock, Sideline Stock, and stock template members that have a zone map**, and **only while the item has zero non-cancelled orders**. Hidden for every other source — items whose design is permanently locked never show it — and hidden the moment the item is ordered. See Epic H.
    - **Send for Approval** — shown only when the store has an Active team manager (Epic B).
    - **Delete Product** — follows the removal rules in Epic C, including the bundle-membership confirmations.
- **The artwork action is deliberately not called "Customize."** That word already names a source type whose items can never be edited after add, so reusing it would invert its meaning on exactly the items that must not offer it.
- **In bundle tag.** Any catalog item that belongs to a bundle carries an **In bundle** tag on its card. The tag is the visible counterpart of the eligibility rule in Epic J: only un-bundled items may join a new bundle, so the tag tells the rep at a glance why an item is not selectable in the Create Bundle picker. The tag names the bundle it belongs to, and clicking through opens that bundle's detail page. The tag clears the moment the item is removed from its bundle.
- **Bundle cards** additionally carry the stacked-thumbnail collage and their price range, and sort alongside products in their category section. A bundle in the **Incomplete** state shows **"Incomplete — needs a second item"** in place of its price range, following the same treatment as an empty category.
- **New category** field at the bottom of the page, with the helper line "Categories become the storefront header tabs, in this order." **Subject to conflict (a) in Governing rule 2** — whether inline creation survives the move to workspace-level categories is unresolved.

**Epic E — Product detail page.** Replaces the former edit drawer entirely, for products and bundles alike. Reached by clicking a catalog card. Header carries **Back to Catalog**, **Delete Product**, and **Save Changes**.

- **Summary rail (left):** product image, product name, source attribution, status chips (source · team setting · visibility · **In bundle**, where applicable), and a quick-reference block — Retail, Category, Applications on file, and application-sizing confirmation state.
- **Essentials:** Product Name, Design ID, Category, Subcategory.
    - **Orphaned products surface the error here too.** Given a product whose category no longer resolves, then the Category field renders empty with an inline error naming the deleted category as the cause, and the summary rail's Category line reports the same. Choosing a category clears the error on save.
- **Product settings:** Team Required and Team Supplied toggles, with the note "Turning either on files this item under the system-managed Team Gear category."
- **Assign to Teams** (League stores): multi-select over the League's child Teams with a selected count, and the note "Team Required and Team Supplied items must stay assigned to at least one Team."
- **Price settings:** the shared price settings card specified in **Governing rule 3** — the **PRICE SETTINGS** header with the segmented `$` / `%` control at its right, a row per size band (**YOUTH**, **ADULT**) carrying read-only Base Cost, the **Dealer Profit** and **Fundraising** inputs with their mode-prefix affordances, the **per-item Fundraising toggle**, and a derived Retail, closing with the **"Retail price visible to customers"** footer line. Where a value was written by bulk pricing, its input carries the provenance note described in Governing rule 3, cleared by typing over it. Team Supplied items show **Covered** in place of the card.
- **Members (bundles only):** the current member products, each with a **Remove** action, plus an **Add items** action opening the Create Bundle picker scoped to **un-bundled catalog items**. Membership is editable until the bundle's first non-cancelled order. Minimum-membership behavior and the Incomplete state are specified in Epic J.
- **Storefront:** **Visibility** toggle ("Live on the storefront once the store launches"); **Allow opt-out** toggle ("I already own this" counts as satisfied in required-gear tracking); and **Performance**, which reports order statistics once the product sells. Sort position is set on the catalog page by dragging, not here.
- **Application Sizing:** see Epic F.

**Epic F — Application sizing.** Every decoration or personalization element on a product is recorded as its own row, so the factory receives an unambiguous spec.

- Columns: **Item** (a thumbnail of the asset or rendered text), **Item Type** (Logo, Team Name, Player Number, Player Name), **Location** (e.g. Front Body, Back Body), **Select Size**, and **Notes**.
- **Select Size** defaults to **As Shown** and is a dropdown per row.
- **Notes** is free text per row, expanding to a bounded number of lines before truncating.
- **Set all to As Shown** applies the default across every row in one action.
- The summary rail reports the count of applications on file and whether sizing is fully confirmed.
- Application sizing is a commercial-lock field: editable until the item's first non-cancelled order.

**Epic G — Stock Vault.** A **platform-level** library, reachable as a dedicated **Stock Vault** page alongside Overview and Stores in the workspace navigation. It does **not** appear as a page inside an individual or League store. It surfaces as a source chip within Add Products, both at Step 2 of store creation and on an existing store's Add Products page.

- **Pro Stock** — stock items sourced from the Prolook brand customizer.
- **Sideline Stock** — stock items sourced from the Prolook brand customizer.
- **These two are the entire vault.** Catalog Templates are **not** part of the Stock Vault; they are a separate source library specified in Epic M.
- **Extraction, not deposit — no exception.** Store products are never saved back into the Stock Vault, and nothing is ever authored inside it. The vault is a read-only catalogue of blanks maintained upstream. (This rule previously carried a single exception for rep-authored Product Packages; that exception is retired now that templates are authored from a store's catalog instead.)
- **Vault items are not categorized by the workspace taxonomy.** Product categories apply to store catalog products only; a vault item carries no category until it is added to a store (*Product Categories*, Governing rule 4). A category deletion therefore never affects the vault.
- **Start a store from the Vault:** the Stock Vault page supports multi-select with a **Start store with selected (n)** bulk action, launching store creation at Step 1 with the selection pre-loaded so Step 2 arrives already populated, shown as completed and review-optional. Pre-loaded items are staged, uncommitted selections and remain subject to the normal per-item confirm.
- **Add to an existing store:** Stock Vault selections can also be added to an existing store from that store's Add Products page.
- **SKU search:** vault surfaces search by name or SKU (placeholder "Search by name or SKU"); a pasted exact SKU returns its item as the top result.
- **Reference pricing:** the Vault's price column shows the item's reference price, visually muted and "ref."-affixed — Stock Vault items carry no committed store pricing.
- **Used in other stores:** a Stock Vault item reports how many stores it is used in, click-through to those stores.

**Epic H — Decoration & the Artwork Editor.** Stock product reaches the storefront decorated, authored by the rep rather than a designer. The tool is the **Artwork Editor** — the single name for it across this spec and the store creation flow.

- **Eligibility.** Only **Pro Stock, Sideline Stock, and stock members of a Catalog Template** are decorated in the Artwork Editor, and only those that have a **zone map**. **Customizable template members** are decorated in the **brand customizer** once they are in the store, not the Artwork Editor. Items whose source is Saved Design, Saved Cart, or Customize — including template members captured from them — are never decorated in either tool; their design is fixed permanently.
- **Two entry points, one editor.**
    - **At add time** — from the per-item configuration modal as the item is added to the store.
    - **After add** — from the **Edit artwork** action in the item's catalog card menu (Epic D), which opens the Artwork Editor **directly** on that item with no intermediate step. This exists so a rep can correct or replace artwork without deleting the product and re-adding it.
- **Re-branding a deployed template member.** Because a template carries the origin store's artwork (Epic M), a stock member deployed into a differently-branded store arrives wearing the wrong marks. The rep corrects this through the ordinary **Edit artwork** path — no special flow. Restricted-source members cannot be corrected this way and never could be.
- **Returning from the editor.** Saving or exiting returns the rep to the store catalog, **scrolled to and focusing the item just edited** — the same pattern as batch commit in Epic A.
- **Decoration locks at first order.** A rep **cannot re-decorate an item with any non-cancelled order**. The Edit artwork action is hidden on ordered items, and decoration sits under the commercial lock in Governing rule 1 alongside price settings and application sizing. Rationale: re-decorating would change gear a player has already bought. Cancelling every order against the item re-unlocks decoration with the other commercial fields.
- **What a placement produces:** two records from one gesture — a *presentation placement* driving storefront renders, and a *production imprint* (zone, physical size, decoration method, colour references, vector asset link) that travels on the order line into the PO / ERP export.
- **Decoration states:** Not decorated → Render pending → Decorated.
- **Render pending:** storefront imagery is a derived, composited artifact. After a placement saves, renders queue server-side; until they land the product shows a render-pending placeholder rather than a broken image.
- **Render gate (first-time decoration):** a decorated product cannot be made **visible** until its render set exists; render-pending items are held with a "renders processing" notice. A visible product can never be imageless; a render failure surfaces to the rep as a retryable error.
- **Re-decorating a live product.** Given a **visible** product whose render set already exists, when the rep saves new artwork, then the **existing render set continues to serve the storefront until the new set lands**, at which point the new renders are swapped in. The product is never pulled from the storefront, never drops to a placeholder, and never appears imageless mid-regeneration. If the new render fails, the **previous set stays live** and the failure surfaces to the rep as a retryable error.
- **Non-decoratable items** — customizer-sourced items, stock without a zone map, and any item carrying a non-cancelled order — never show decoration affordances anywhere, including the card menu.

**Epic I — Team artwork library.** The team's single logo becomes a managed library of marks (primary, wordmark, alternates, sport variants), each carrying a **production-readiness state** (Production-ready / Needs vector), so an unvalidated mark is visibly not-yet-usable rather than silently wrong at the factory. The **Artwork Editor** and the brand customizer read from this library; store-recents become a view over it, not the storage. The library lives on the **Team** and persists across seasons.

**Epic J — Product bundles.** Bundles are rep-authored and **buyer-facing**: a bundle is a storefront line item. They are distinct from Catalog Templates, which are rep-facing and never seen by a shopper. Bundle type is selected at creation and is **immutable after creation** (pre-order, delete and recreate to change). Created from the catalog page via **+ Create Bundle**.

- **All-Items Bundle** — the shopper purchases every member item in one action for one combined price. Best for complete uniforms or kits.
- **Choice Bundle ("Optional Groupings")** — the shopper selects **exactly one** member item (e.g. full-length or cuffed pant). Price is the selected option's; where prices differ, catalog and storefront show **"from $X"**. Team Supplied choice bundles display Covered.
- **Shared rules:** non-empty bundle name; **at least 2 member products**; **only un-bundled catalog items are eligible** — an item already in another bundle cannot join a second one, and a bundle can only be built from products already in this store's catalog (anything else is added via Add Products first); distinction (TR/TS) set explicitly at creation, never inherited; stacked-thumbnail collage card; bundles enter the catalog as live records, hidden by default like any product. A TR/TS bundle is filed under Team Gear and, in a League store, requires team assignment; a non-team bundle requires a category at creation.
- **A bundle can be orphaned like any other catalog item.** A bundle carries a category, so deleting that category orphans the bundle itself — its members keep their own categories and are unaffected unless they shared the deleted one. An orphaned bundle stays purchasable and keeps its Visibility; it is re-filed exactly as a product is. This is independent of the Incomplete state, and a bundle can be both.
- **Member visibility prompt.** At creation, after members are chosen, the rep is asked whether to **hide the individual member items from the store**.
    - Choosing to hide sets each member's **Visibility** toggle off, so the bundle becomes the only way to buy that gear.
    - Choosing to keep them visible leaves each member's visibility untouched; the items remain individually purchasable alongside the bundle.
    - The prompt lists the items it will affect and notes the choice is reversible from each item's detail page.
    - This writes the existing per-product Visibility flag — it is a convenience, not a new visibility concept, and members hidden this way are **not** locked. The rep may turn any of them back on individually at any time.
- **Bundle price settings:** a bundle carries **one profit set rather than a row per size band** — **Dealer Profit** and **Fundraising** — with a derived Youth / Adult base-to-retail readout in the footer. This single-row structure is the only structural exception to Governing rule 3; the card is otherwise identical, including the **PRICE SETTINGS** header with its segmented `$` / `%` control, the prefix affordances on each input, percent resolved against base cost, and clear-on-switch.
- **A bundle carries its own Fundraising toggle.** It behaves exactly as a product's does — defaults to the store's Team Fundraising setting, preserves its amount when switched off, is subject to the store-level flag, and locks with the bundle's other commercial fields at first non-cancelled order.
- **Bundle fundraising replaces member fundraising on the bundle line; it does not stack.** This mirrors the existing treatment of dealer profit, so the card composes both of its inputs by the same rule. Given a bundle whose members each carry their own fundraising amount, when a shopper buys the bundle, then the fundraising collected is **the bundle's figure only** — member fundraising is ignored on that line. Member fundraising continues to govern those items **when sold individually**. Stacking was considered and rejected: with members carrying per-band fundraising and the bundle carrying a single figure, a stacked total would resolve to a range rather than a number, would shift silently when membership changed, and would differ per selection on a Choice Bundle.
- **A member's fundraising toggle does not affect the bundle line.** Because the bundle's figure replaces its members', a member with fundraising switched off contributes nothing to and subtracts nothing from the bundle's fundraising. This is intended, not a defect.
- **Bundle configuration options:** **Visibility**, **Hide retail price** (display flag; the shopper still pays at checkout), and **Free shipping for the bundle**. There is deliberately no "$0" option — Team Supplied covers the no-charge case. Visibility and Hide retail price are editable in any lock state, except where the Incomplete-bundle rule holds Visibility off; Free shipping locks with commercial fields at first order.
- **Bundle Pricing Information:** a read-only, live-recalculating summary in both the create panel and the bundle's detail page — Store price range, **Fundraising range**, Dealer profit range, Customer price range, and Est. shipping (shown only when free shipping is on, reading "Free"). The Fundraising range reports **the bundle's own figure**, consistent with the replace rule above, and is omitted entirely when the bundle's fundraising is off. All-Items bundles collapse each range to a single value; Choice Bundles show true ranges. The catalog card derives from this block, and a Choice Bundle's "from $X" equals the customer-price low end.
- **Editing membership after creation.** Membership is **editable until the bundle's first non-cancelled order**, from the **Members** section of the bundle detail page (Epic E). The rep may add un-bundled catalog items and remove existing members. Bundle **type** remains immutable from creation. Any non-cancelled order locks membership along with the rest of the bundle's structure.
- **Minimum membership & the Incomplete state.** A bundle needs two members to reach the storefront, but a rep mid-edit is never blocked from removing one — reps swap members routinely, and blocking the removal would turn every swap into a delete-and-recreate.
    - Removing a member that would leave the bundle with **one** member is **permitted**. The bundle enters the **Incomplete** state.
    - In the Incomplete state, the bundle's **Visibility is forced off and held off**; its catalog card reads **"Incomplete — needs a second item"** in place of its price range; it is excluded from the storefront feed; and it cannot satisfy the launch gate's at-least-one-visible-product requirement.
    - The **Members** section surfaces the same state inline, alongside an **Add items** action and a **Delete bundle instead** action.
    - Adding a second member clears the Incomplete state and returns Visibility to the rep's control, defaulting to off as with any newly eligible record.
    - **A bundle is never auto-dissolved.** Name, type, distinction, category, team assignment, sort position, profit set, price mode, fundraising toggle and amount, Hide retail price, and Free shipping all survive the Incomplete state intact.
    - Because membership locks at first non-cancelled order, the Incomplete state can only ever occur on a bundle with **zero** non-cancelled orders. No live order is ever affected by it.
    - A removed member returns to **un-bundled** status: it loses its **In bundle** tag and becomes eligible for other bundles. If it had been hidden by the member-visibility prompt, it **stays hidden** — the rep restores it deliberately from its detail page.
    - The Incomplete state is a **gate on visibility, not a draft state.** It follows the established pattern of the render gate (Epic H) and empty-category suppression (Governing rule 2). Catalog records remain live throughout; there is still no draft or published state for any catalog item.
- **Type-specific rules:** a Team Required Choice Bundle is satisfied by purchasing any one member. A choice-bundle order records the specific member selected at SKU/design granularity; an all-items order fans out to all members. Any non-cancelled order locks the bundle structure (name, type, membership, distinction) and all restricted-source members including unordered options; Stock Vault members follow their source policy; bundles otherwise inherit the strictest member's policy. Choice bundles may contain made-to-order members, but the create panel warns: "Splitting a required purchase across options may prevent individual options from reaching minimum order quantity."

**Epic K — Bulk pricing.** A modal lets a rep set markups across many products at once.

- **Inputs:** **Dealer Profit** and **Fundraising**, rendered through the shared price settings component with its segmented `$` / `%` control governing both fields (Governing rule 3). Team Profit is removed from this modal along with everything else.
- **Mode is written to every target.** The mode selected in the modal applies to each targeted product, overwriting that product's existing mode along with its values. In `%` mode each percentage resolves against **each size band's own base cost** on each product, so one entry yields correct per-band figures across differing costs.
- **Bulk pricing never turns fundraising on.** The per-item Fundraising toggle stays authoritative. A fundraising amount entered here is written **only to targets that already have fundraising enabled** — that is, the store's Team Fundraising flag is on and the item's own toggle is on. Targets with fundraising off receive the dealer profit and the mode, and their preserved fundraising amount is left untouched. The Fundraising input is hidden from the modal entirely when Team Fundraising is off at store level.
- **The apply is asymmetric, and says so.** Because the two inputs reach different numbers of products, the confirmation reports **both counts** — dealer profit and mode written to *n* products, fundraising written to *m* — rather than a single total. A rep who expects a fundraising sweep and gets a partial one should learn that from the modal, not from spot-checking the catalog.
- **Scope toggles (mutually exclusive):** "Apply pricing to all products" and "Apply pricing to selected products."
- **All-products mode:** the entered markups are set on every **eligible** product in the store. Orphaned products are eligible like any other — a missing category has no bearing on pricing.
- **Selected-products mode:** a product picker (mirroring the Create Bundle picker) lets the rep choose which eligible products to re-price.
- **Eligibility:** Stock Vault-sourced items are always eligible; restricted-source items are eligible only while they have zero non-cancelled orders. Locked products are excluded from the all-products scope and disabled in the picker with the lock indicator — consistent with the commercial lock in Governing rule 1.
- **Apply:** **Set Pricing** writes the markups and mode to every size-band row on all targeted products; each retail recalculates as base cost + dealer profit + fundraising, with percentages resolved against that row's base cost.
- **Every written value is marked.** Values set here carry a **bulk-pricing provenance note** on the corresponding input in that item's price settings (Governing rule 3), so a rep opening a product later can tell a swept figure from a deliberate one. The note is cleared by typing over the value or by switching the item's `$` / `%` mode, and is re-set by a subsequent bulk apply — including over a manual override.

- The bulk pricing modal carries an **Apply to bundles** toggle. Off by default: bulk pricing  
applies only to individual products in the selection, and any bundles in the selection are  
skipped and named as excluded in the confirmation. On: bulk pricing applies to bundles in the  
selection as well.
- The toggle does not persist between uses. Every bulk pricing action starts with it off.
- The confirmation summary states how many products and how many bundles the change will affect  
before it is applied.

**Epic L — Team assignment & league-wide availability (League stores).** Built on the flattened parent → child Team structure. Team assignment applies to League stores only; Single-Team Locker stores have one inherent Team, so no assignment control is shown.

- **Assign to Teams:** a multi-select over the League's child Teams, binding the product to each Team's **team code**.
- **TR/TS assignment is mandatory:** setting TR or TS with no Team assigned prompts required assignment before saving; a TR/TS item can never exist unassigned in a League store. Enforced on the product detail page, the per-item configuration modal, and the pending-products commit.
- **Zero-team stores:** if the store has no Teams when TR/TS is set, the prompt offers **inline Team creation** (team name plus auto-generated team code). The prompt lists only that store's teams, never teams from other stores.
- **League-wide default:** an unassigned item is league-wide and by definition has no team setting — available to all shoppers with no code. An item assigned to every Team displays as league-wide but retains per-team-code scoping.
- **Storefront outcome:** a buyer entering a team code sees all TR/TS gear assigned to that Team plus league-wide items — no pre-filled roster required.
- **Assignment cannot be reduced below one Team** while TR/TS is on. Individual team stores are exempt. A league store with no teams has an empty Roster Bank — there is no store-wide capture backlog, because team-gear captures derive from team-assigned orders and a store with no teams can have none.

**Epic M — Catalog Templates.** A Catalog Template is a rep-authored, reusable set of gear captured from a store's existing catalog. It replaces Product Packages, which served the same purpose but were authored inside the vault from pristine sources. Templates are **rep-facing** and never seen by a shopper — that is the line between a template and a bundle (Epic J).

**Creating a template**

- **Authored from a store catalog, not from the vault.** On the Products tab the rep multi-selects one, several, or all items in the catalog (Epic D, Selection mode) and chooses **Create Catalog Template**. There is no other creation path; templates cannot be assembled from the Stock Vault, from the Customizer, or from scratch.
- **Naming:** the rep supplies a non-empty template name. The template records its origin store and creation date.
- **Bundles come along.** A bundle may be selected into a template. Selecting a bundle **implicitly selects its member items**, since a bundle cannot exist without them. The template stores the bundle as a bundle — its type, membership, profit set, price mode, fundraising toggle state, Hide retail price, and Free shipping all captured — alongside its members.
- **The capture notice.** Before the template is saved, the rep is shown which selected items carry a **permanent design lock** — anything sourced from a Saved Design, Saved Cart, or Customize. The notice states plainly that those items keep this store's artwork wherever the template is deployed and **can never be re-branded**, and names them individually rather than giving a count. The rep may proceed or go back and deselect them.
- **Orphaned products can be captured.** A product with no resolvable category may be selected into a template; category is re-confirmed on deploy in any case, so the missing value is supplied there. The template records no category for that member.

**What a template captures**

- **The origin store's branding.** A template is a **branded snapshot**. Each member carries the artwork, logos, and team colors as configured in the origin store at the moment of capture — it does **not** revert to a style block. This is the deliberate inverse of the **Other stores** source in Epic A, which adds an item at its style block precisely so the rep starts clean.
- **Source type travels.** Each member retains the source type it had in the origin store, so edit-locking behaves identically after deploy (Governing rule 1).
- **What is re-confirmed on deploy:** pricing — including the fundraising toggle and amount — team setting, sizes, category, and team assignment are re-confirmed on every deploy through the normal per-item configuration modal. Artwork is not.
- **A snapshot, not a live link.** The template is fixed at creation. Later edits to the origin store — re-decoration, price changes, deletion of a captured product — do not propagate to the template, and the template does not propagate back.
- **Categories are not captured as records.** A template stores each member's category **by reference to the workspace taxonomy**, not as a copy. A template deployed after its captured category has been deleted presents that member with no category pre-selected, and the rep chooses one in the configuration modal. A template can never recreate a deleted category.

**Deploying a template**

- **As a source in Add Products.** Templates appear under the **Catalog Templates** chip (Epic A). Selecting a template stages all of its members; each is then configured individually like any other staged item.
- **Members land as individual catalog items.** The template identity does not persist in the destination store — there is no "this came from a template" state on a product. A captured bundle deploys as a bundle, subject to the ordinary bundle rules including the two-member minimum.
- **Artwork lands as captured.** Deployed members arrive wearing the origin store's branding. In a store for the same team, this is the point of the feature. In a differently-branded store, the rep re-decorates stock members through **Edit artwork** (Epic H); restricted-source members cannot be re-branded at all.
- **Fundraising is re-resolved against the destination store.** A member whose toggle was on in the origin store deploys into a store with Team Fundraising off with its fundraising suppressed and its amount preserved, per Governing rule 3. Nothing about a template overrides the destination store's setting.
- **Starting a store from a template.** A rep starts a store from a template by creating the store and selecting the template at **Step 2 — Add Products**, which presents the same source chips as the in-store Add Products page. There is no interstitial template-choice screen and no template path off Add Store; **Add Store still enters Step 1 directly.** One template per store creation is supported; multi-template selection is out of scope for this version.
- **No workspace navigation surface.** Catalog Templates are **not** a workspace nav tab and have no dedicated library page. They are reachable only as a source within Add Products. Renaming and deleting a template are handled from the template card's overflow menu in that view.

**Boundaries**

- A template is not a store template. Store templates were removed from the product in August 2026 and are not being reintroduced — a template populates a catalog, and carries no store settings, schedule, roster, or branding configuration of its own.
- A template is not a bundle. Bundles are buyer-facing storefront line items; templates are never rendered to a shopper.
- The Stock Vault is not involved. Creating a template writes nothing to the vault, and the vault's pure-extraction rule (Epic G) is unaffected.

**Epic N — Batch decoration (Decorate Selected → review grid).**

- Given multi-select in the catalog, when the rep chooses **Decorate Selected (n)**, then the  
Artwork Editor opens in catalog-wide mode: one placement — artwork, zone, size, method — applied  
across the product set and resolved per product through each template's imprint-zone map, so  
"left chest, 3.5 in" means the right thing on a hoodie and a polo alike.
- A **review grid** shows every resolved result as **Resolved** or **Needs review** (no zone map on  
that template). Each card can be excluded, included, or overridden individually before commit.
- **Save all** creates one decorated item per included product: placement records persist and  
server renders queue. The count is confirmed by toast.
- Batch decoration is subject to Governing rule 1. Items already carrying orders are excluded from  
the selection and named in the review grid as locked.

Requires imprint-zone maps authored per product template, the server-side compositor with render  
queueing, and multi-select in the catalog with a bulk action bar.

### Metrics & Success Criteria

- Config-modal accuracy — post-add commercial edits near zero.
- **Zero edit or delete actions against ordered made-to-order items.**
- **Zero re-decoration attempts against ordered items** (enforced by hiding the action).
- Share of stock items re-decorated from the catalog after add — a read on whether the Edit artwork entry point is earning itself, and on how often add-time decoration is getting it wrong.
- Storefront impressions of a placeholder or missing image during re-decoration = zero (enforced by serving the previous render set).
- **Share of categories whose product order has been manually changed** — a read on whether rep-controlled sorting is used or whether the append default is good enough.
- **Storefront engagement with the first product in a category** vs. the rest — whether pinning actually moves behavior.
- TR/TS items with missing team assignment = zero (enforced, League stores).
- **Store items with no resolvable category** — **no longer zero, and no longer enforceable.** This metric previously read "uncategorized store items = zero (enforced)", which held while categories were store-managed and deletion demanded reassignment. A workspace-level deletion can now orphan products, so the figure to watch is the **count of orphaned products and how long they stay orphaned**. Save-time enforcement is unchanged: a product still cannot be *created* without a category.
- **Time from a category deletion to the last affected product being re-filed**, and the share never re-filed — the direct read on whether warn-and-orphan is sustainable, and the evidence for or against a bulk re-file path.
- **Storefront sessions that encounter an orphaned product** via search or direct link while it is absent from category browsing — the measurable form of the degradation the state accepts.
- Stores launched with at least one visible product = 100% (enforced by the launch gate).
- Products left hidden at store launch — tracked as a signal that the visibility default is costing reps rather than protecting them.
- Split of price entry between `$` and `%` mode, by product type — a read on whether percent mode earns its surface area.
- Mode switches per item before first save — a high count suggests the clear-on-switch behavior is costing reps re-entry.
- Products where Youth and Adult markups differ — a read on whether per-band entry is being used or whether reps enter the same figure twice.
- **Share of items with fundraising individually disabled in a fundraising-enabled store** — the read on whether per-item control was the missing capability or a rarely-used one. A near-zero rate means the store-level toggle was sufficient.
- **Items whose fundraising was disabled and later re-enabled**, and whether the preserved amount was kept or changed on re-enable — a read on whether value preservation is doing real work.
- **Stores sitting in the mixed state** (Team Fundraising off with ordered items still collecting), and how long they stay there.
- **Bulk-priced values later overridden at the item level**, split by Dealer Profit and Fundraising — a high rate means the bulk defaults are wrong, and the provenance note is what makes the override visible.
- **Bulk applies where the fundraising count materially trailed the dealer-profit count** — a read on whether reps understand that bulk pricing does not switch fundraising on.
- Bundles left in the **Incomplete** state for more than one session — the signal that removal is being used where delete-bundle was meant.
- Bundle membership edits per bundle pre-order — a measure of whether post-creation editing removed the delete-and-recreate workaround.
- **Templates created per rep, and the share of catalogs that get captured at all** — the first read on whether authoring-from-catalog is discoverable.
- **Template reuse rate** — share of templates deployed to two or more stores, and the split between same-team redeploys and cross-team ones.
- **Re-decoration rate on deployed template members** — a high rate means templates are being used cross-team and the branded-snapshot decision is costing reps work.
- **Deploys that include a restricted-source member into a differently-branded store** — the failure case the capture notice exists to prevent; should trend toward zero.
- Median time from store creation to first visible product, template path vs. manual path.
- Share of adds sourced from Other stores — a measure of whether cross-store reuse is worth its surface area.
- League products correctly scoped to team codes.
- Choice-bundle option-selection completion rate; MOQ shortfall rate on choice-bundle options.
- Category and subcategory usage in storefront nav engagement.
- Zero imageless visible products.

### Dependencies & Technical Constraints

This feature requires: a persisted **per-product source-type attribute** driving the edit policy, preserved across template capture and deploy so a restricted-source item cannot be laundered into an editable one; an **order-existence check** per product (any non-cancelled order), consumed by the commercial lock, the removal rules, the **Edit artwork** card-menu gate, and the **fundraising-disable exemption**; **MOQ data** on made-to-order items; a **visibility flag** defaulting to off, read by the catalog feed resolver and by the launch gate, and overridable by the render gate and the Incomplete-bundle gate; **category and subcategory persistence** with a single-category constraint per product, rep-controlled ordering, removal handling, Team Gear system-locking, and TR/TS-to-Team-Gear derivation, feeding storefront tab resolution and empty-category suppression — **all of which is subject to the unresolved workspace-level conflicts flagged in Governing rule 2**; a **per-product sort position scoped to its category (and subcategory where present)**, writable by drag on the catalog page, read by the storefront as display order within a tab, appended on add, retained through hide/show, through the Incomplete-bundle state, and through the orphaned state, editable in any lock state as a placement field, and re-based when an item changes category.

**Products reference categories by id, not by name.** This is what makes a workspace-level rename non-destructive and a workspace-level deletion detectable. It also means a recreated category is a different record, so orphaned products never silently reattach.

The **orphaned-category state** requires a **first-class, queryable condition** — a product whose category id does not resolve against the workspace taxonomy — rather than a null discovered at render time. Three consumers depend on being able to find these products by query: the catalog page's Uncategorized section and error treatment; the **addressable Uncategorized filter**; and **C-27** on *Store Home*, which is specified to read this query rather than scan for unresolved ids itself. A fourth consumer, the storefront feed, must treat an unresolved category as **absent from category placement but present in the catalog** — serving the product by direct link and name search while excluding it from tabs, category filters, and category search. Each consumer interpreting a null for itself would diverge; the condition must be resolved in one place.

Pricing requires a **single shared price settings component** implementing Governing rule 3 — **two markup inputs (Dealer Profit and Fundraising) with Team Profit removed from the data model as well as the UI**; per-size-band rows for products and a single row for bundles; a read-only base cost and derived retail per row; a section-scoped `$` / `%` mode persisted per item and defaulting to `$`; prefix affordances driven by that mode; clear-on-switch semantics; per-row percentage resolution against that row's base cost rounded to the cent before summing; and a **per-item fundraising flag** — consumed unmodified by the product detail page, the per-item configuration modal, the Create Bundle panel, and bulk pricing, so the four surfaces cannot drift.

The **fundraising model** requires: a **per-item boolean** initialised from the store's Team Fundraising flag and independently editable thereafter; **rendering gated on the conjunction** of the store flag and the item flag; **amount persistence independent of both flags**, so a disabled item retains its figure indefinitely; **prior-state retention across a store-level disable**, so re-enabling restores each item's own flag rather than switching every item on; and an **exemption path for commercially-locked items** such that a store-level disable cannot clear the flag on an item carrying a non-cancelled order. The store-level control must therefore resolve a **three-state read** — enabled, disabled, and disabled-with-exemptions — rather than a boolean, and must expose the exempted item set for display (see *Store General Settings*, Epic A4). Any surface computing retail must treat the fundraising term as conditional per item rather than per store.

**Bulk pricing** requires a write path that targets dealer profit and mode across all eligible products while targeting fundraising **only** at products whose fundraising is already enabled, returning **two separate affected counts** for the confirmation. It also requires **per-item, per-field provenance persistence** recording that a value originated from a bulk apply, cleared on manual entry into that field and on a mode switch, re-set by a subsequent apply, and read by the price settings component to render the provenance note. Provenance must be stored rather than inferred, so it survives reload.

Also required: **application-sizing records** (asset reference, item type, location, size selection, notes) reaching the PO / ERP export; **Stock Vault persistence at platform scope** spanning Pro Stock and Sideline Stock only, read-only with no write path from any store, with SKU-indexed search, reference-price display, used-in-stores counts, and the start-store pre-load path into Step 1; **Catalog Template persistence at rep scope**, storing a name, origin store, creation timestamp, and a frozen per-member snapshot comprising the member's source type, style reference, and **resolved artwork and imprint records as configured in the origin store**, plus captured bundle structures (type, membership, profit set, price mode, fundraising state, display flags) — immutable after creation, with no live link to the origin store in either direction, referencing categories by id rather than copying them, and with no workspace navigation surface; a **catalog multi-select model** on the Products tab spanning category sections with select-all, scoped to template creation and — if the open item in Governing rule 2 is taken — bulk re-filing; a **restricted-source detection pass** at template creation that resolves and names the permanently-locked members for the capture notice; **cross-store product lookup** scoped to the rep's own stores for the Other stores source, returning each item at its **style block** rather than its customized state — the deliberate inverse of template capture; a **unified Add Products query** returning all four sources into one grid with per-source counts, brand-scoped for the Customizer source only; the **Artwork Editor** writing placement and imprint records, reachable from both the add-time configuration modal and the catalog card menu with a **card-menu visibility gate keyed to source type, zone-map presence, and order state**, plus a return path that re-enters the catalog scrolled to and focusing the edited item; **imprint-zone maps** authored per product template; a **server-side compositor** with a hash-keyed render cache, invalidation on artwork or placement change, a **retain-previous-render-set-until-swap** policy so a live product never serves a placeholder during re-decoration, and a render-failure retry surface that leaves the prior set live; a **team artwork library** on the Team entity with production-readiness states, read by both the Artwork Editor and the brand customizer; **bundle-type and bundle-pricing persistence** including the bundle's own fundraising flag and amount, plus member-level order lines for choice bundles, with the bundle's fundraising figure **replacing** rather than summing member figures on the bundle line; a **bundle-membership index per catalog item** driving the In bundle tag, Create Bundle picker eligibility, and the removal warnings in Epic C; **pre-order membership mutability** on bundles with an order-state lock; an **Incomplete-bundle flag** derived from member count that forces visibility off, excludes the bundle from the storefront feed, and disqualifies it from the launch gate; **product-to-Team mapping** re-keyed to parent → child Teams with team-code binding; **validation at every save surface** for required category and TR/TS team assignment; and PDP/checkout identity-capture fields flowing to the Roster Bank, with PDP jersey-number entry reading the target roster's duplicate-number policy and taken-number set (Roster Management, Epic 4).

Reporting consumes the fundraising line items produced here: the store-scoped **Fundraising Report** and **Team Revenue** chart (*Store Analytics (Reports Tab)*) and the workspace **Dealer Profit** card, which reports gross revenue net of COGS, platform fee, sales tax, and team fundraising. No reporting surface depends on Team Profit any longer.

Retained from the platform baseline: the rep's **brand entitlement list**; per-account persistence of **Saved Designs** and **Saved Carts** scoped to the selected brand; the sport/product template catalog and builder engine for the Customize New Product wizard; and shared internal catalog, order, and roster services plus brand-customizer integration.
